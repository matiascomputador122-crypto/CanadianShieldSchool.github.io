A landscape on a grand scale

At the centre of a continent

The Shield is the exposed ancient core of North America. Its Canadian reaches curve around Hudson Bay and include broad stretches of northern Ontario and Quebec, Manitoba, Labrador, Nunavut and the Northwest Territories.

  • More than 1.8 million km² within Canada
  • Four regions: Kazan, Davis, James and Laurentian
  • Ontario gateway cities marked on the map

Extent: Geological Survey of Canada. Region map: The Atlas of Canada.

The story written in stone

A landscape shaped over deep time

The Shield's bedrock and lake-filled basins carry clues from a very ancient chapter of Earth's history.

01

Fire & pressure

Volcanic activity, shifting plates and mountain building created and transformed the crust. Much of the bedrock is crystalline igneous or metamorphic rock, including granite and gneiss.

02

Mountains worn low

Over immense spans of time, uplift, weathering and erosion wore down old mountain belts, leaving rounded rock ridges and broad areas of exposed bedrock.

03

Ice leaves its signature

Continental glaciers scraped and polished the rock. Retreating ice left thin soils, rocky shores, wetlands and countless lake basins.

Look closely: Resistant bedrock, glacial deposits and forests rooted in thin soils make the Shield's mix of rock, water and green.

Geology: Government of Ontario; Natural Resources Canada, Atlas of Canada.

Wild edges, wide horizons

Minerals · knowledge · work

Rich ground, working north

Ancient rocks hold gold, nickel, copper, iron ore and other minerals. Mining is a major industry; forestry, hydroelectricity and tourism also support Shield communities.

Two unforgettable landmarks

Save these for the camera roll
⌁

Sleeping Giant Provincial Park

Giant-shaped cliffs rise over Lake Superior. More than 100 km of trails reach exposed rock, forest, quiet bays and broad lookouts.

↟

Kakabeka Falls

A dramatic 40 m waterfall pours into a deep gorge. The rim boardwalk offers year-round views of the falls and ancient rocks below.

Park facts: Ontario Parks, Sleeping Giant; Ontario Parks, Kakabeka Falls.
